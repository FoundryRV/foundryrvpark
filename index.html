The Park

Built for working crews and long-haul travelers alike.

Memphis sits at the crossroads of US Highway 287 and State Highway 256, the county seat of Hall County and one of the last real stops between Amarillo and Wichita Falls. It is a town of brick-paved streets, a 1923 classical-revival courthouse on the square, and a whole lot of open sky.

Foundry is being built for the way people actually travel through here — energy and construction crews on multi-month assignments, snowbirds running 287, and families who would rather not fight a tight, tree-choked lot after 400 miles of driving. Every site is the same generous size, every site is full hookup, and the drive lanes are wide enough that you will not be backing into anybody's awning.

  • Level, all-weather asphalt throughout — no gravel dust, no mud
  • Perimeter drive lanes so you enter and exit without a three-point turn
  • Big-rig friendly: 40′+ coaches with a towed car fit inside the site
  • Nightly, weekly, and monthly stays

Sites & Hookups

Thirty sites. One size. All of them big.

The park is laid out on a single spine running the long axis of the lot, with sites projecting straight out to either side and drive lanes on both outside edges. It is a simple plan, and simple plans are easy to maneuver.

Site dimensions

25′ × 75′

Every site, front to back. Enough length for a 40-foot coach plus a tow vehicle, and enough width that your slides and awning are not in your neighbor's living room.

Power & utilities

30 / 50amp

Metered pedestal at every site with 30-amp and 50-amp service, potable water, and a sewer connection at the pad. No sharing, no adapters hunting.

Access

2drive lanes

Full-length lanes on both outside edges of the lot with cross drives and turnaround aprons at each end. Back in from the outside, hook up toward the center.

Amenities

The things that actually matter after a long day.

  • Full hookups at every site

    30-amp and 50-amp power, potable water, and sewer at the pad. Nothing shared, nothing improvised.

  • Laundry on site

    Washers and dryers in the service building so a month-long stay does not mean a weekly trip to town.

  • Restrooms & hot showers

    A clean, well-lit bathhouse — because sometimes you want a shower you can turn around in.

  • Park-wide Wi-Fi

    Coverage across the lot for remote work, streaming, and keeping in touch from the middle of the Panhandle.

  • Pet area

    A fenced run so the dogs get to stretch out too. Pet friendly throughout the park.

  • Long-term & workforce stays

    Monthly arrangements built for crews working locally. Ask about blocks of sites and billing.

While you are here

  • Hall County Heritage Hall — local history and natural science exhibits in the old First National Bank building on the square (Texas Historical Commission).
  • The 1923 courthouse — Classical Revival with Corinthian columns, still the working seat of Hall County (Texas Time Travel).
  • Brookhollow Country Club Lake — private fishing lake about six miles northeast of town (Wikipedia).
  • Caprock Canyons & Palo Duro — the Panhandle's two great canyon parks are both an easy day trip west.
